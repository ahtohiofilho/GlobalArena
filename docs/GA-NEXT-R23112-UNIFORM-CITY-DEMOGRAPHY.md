# R231.12 — Uniform city demographic growth

Base #680 2cf2513, CI approved and Windows playtest reached turn 100. Approved production design: one initial worker per civilization; one birth per civilization per turn if at least one eligible occupied physical node exists. Uniform draw among distinct nodes with at least one stationary own worker at the end of the settled turn. No population or previous-birth weighting, parent selection, inheritance or companion travel. A moving worker or pending edge crossing is excluded; an unfinished active move is excluded even at an intermediate edge boundary. For an active build approached in the last traveling interval, defer eligibility until the following stationary interval. A node with a stationary own worker remains eligible even if another worker is traveling. If none are eligible, skip the birth, with no carried credit. Newborns remain at the selected node, start next turn with empty own orders and automatic local activity. Existing economic presence accounting during travel is unchanged.

Explicit new-game preview GA_NEXT_R23112_UNIFORM_CITY_DEMOGRAPHY=1 requires one-worker opening and is mutually exclusive with the legacy weighted individual mode. A canonical uniform policy marker (not a counter) identifies v26; legacy R164 and R231.9 v25 histories and existing QA scenarios remain available without migration. Deterministic unbiased bounded draw keyed by settled hash, verified calendar digest, turn and owner; canonical node/owner order. Replay recomputes the actual birth batch. No new money, trade, allocation or movement rules.

Acceptance: nine stationary real Godot closes, 10 workers per owner, exact replay, same economics/cartography gates; 256 pure counterfactual draws across 2:1-population sites with equal expected probabilities, transit-only site and all-travel owner/all-travel world exclusion; real E-close orders moving all 10 human workers across an ordinary 1.4-turn land edge, skip human birth in crossing turn then resume at destination with no carried credit.
